# CEDIA monochrome icon research — 2026-09-28

## Scope and conclusion

This note records first-party visual and trademark guidance from OpenAI/ChatGPT and Notion, then translates the useful visual principles into six original CEDIA directions. The reference brands are useful for studying restraint, geometry, negative space, and monochrome presentation. Their marks must not be traced, recombined, or used as the CEDIA identity: both companies prohibit confusingly similar logos and imitation of their visual identity.

The safest CEDIA brief is therefore: one self-contained black/white mark with a consistent rounded stroke, a deliberate open void, and a meaning tied to CEDIA's IDE, agent handoff, OMP relay, and routing surfaces. Keep any OpenAI or Notion provider mark as a separate, exact, secondary integration label when its use is permitted. This is design guidance, not trademark clearance.

## Current CEDIA baseline

The repository already records an owner-approved D-terminal mark in [`assets/brand/cedia-terminal-d-v1/README.md`](../../../../assets/brand/cedia-terminal-d-v1/README.md) and the dated adoption receipt in [`cedia-d-terminal-brand-2026-09-26/findings.md`](../cedia-d-terminal-brand-2026-09-26/findings.md). That baseline is a flat monochrome silhouette with no glow or shadow; light and dark exports are exact color inversions generated from the approved reference. The six directions below should therefore be treated as an exploration board for an explicit new choice, not as an implicit replacement of the adopted mark.

## OpenAI / ChatGPT: what the first-party guide says

Source: [OpenAI Design Guidelines](https://openai.com/brand/) (accessed 2026-09-28).

- OpenAI says its name, logo, ChatGPT and GPT brands, icons, and design elements are OpenAI marks. The guide is intended for third parties using those marks in their own materials.
- The Blossom description identifies a visual idea, not a reusable construction recipe: circles convey the fluidity and warmth of human-centered thinking; right angles convey the precision and structure of technology. This is the useful conceptual reference for CEDIA, while the actual Blossom geometry remains OpenAI's mark.
- The wordmark uses OpenAI Sans and a perfect-circle “O”; its balance and proportions are fixed. The guide says not to stretch, alter, crop, mask, texture, or use unapproved variations.
- For Blossom, the guide says not to add color or unauthorized elements, not to use it as the primary branding, and to give it prescribed spacing and lots of open space. The page says to use the mark exactly as provided and acknowledge that it belongs to OpenAI.
- OpenAI permits a logo only when directly related to OpenAI services and says not to imply endorsement, sponsorship, or a relationship that does not exist. It also says not to make OpenAI's mark more prominent than the user's own mark, place it on merchandise, incorporate it into a new brand/trademark, or design a similar logo.
- Partnership lockups require balanced spacing, an approved color format, no distortion, similar wordmark height, and approval from both brands. A CEDIA provider row should follow the same conservative hierarchy only when a formal co-brand use case exists.
- The guide states that app titles must not use model names and that the GPT brand is not permitted in app, product, developer, or company names. CEDIA should remain “CEDIA” and describe any model/provider relationship separately and accurately.

The source does **not** prescribe a CEDIA-like line weight or a general black-and-white icon system. “Consistent rounded stroke, open void, and no effects” below is an implementation recommendation inferred from the guide's geometry, color, spacing, and alteration rules—not an OpenAI requirement.

## Notion: what the first-party material says

Sources: [Notion Trademark Usage Guidelines](https://www.notion.so/Notion-Trademark-Usage-Guidelines-9826313c686a4f6e9d8a48347162714b), [Notion's brand usage guidelines](https://notion.notion.site/Notion-s-brand-usage-guidelines-How-to-use-Notion-s-brand-in-your-marketing-30a5510bc5644475a28844e427008bee), [Notion Brand Guidelines](https://notion.notion.site/Notion-Brand-Guidelines-db8fda2d1f0048bba1f4e547dfc48830), and [Notion Media Kit](https://notion.notion.site/Media-Kit-205535b1d9c4440497a3d7a2ac096286) (accessed 2026-09-28).

- Notion identifies the “Notion” wordmark and its black-and-white cube logo as valuable Notion Marks. The logo file should come from Notion's media kit, not a redraw or an icon extracted from a screenshot.
- A third party may refer to Notion marks accurately to explain a relationship such as “works with,” “built with,” or “Notion integration,” and may use only marks approved for that use case. Permission can be modified or revoked.
- Notion prohibits implying endorsement, sponsorship, partnership, or affiliation that does not exist; putting “Notion” in a business, app, website, domain, or social handle; copying Notion's look and feel; or making a confusingly similar logo, identity, name, or slogan. It also prohibits monetizing Notion marks or badges without a license.
- Where permission has been granted, Notion requires an ownership attribution. The exact attribution supplied by the guideline is: “Notion and the Notion logo are trademarks of Notion Labs, Inc., and are used here with permission.”
- Notion's marketing page says to lead with the creator's own brand and keep a “Made for Notion” or “Made with Notion” badge secondary to the main image/message, one badge per layout. This supports a CEDIA-primary, provider-secondary hierarchy for any approved integration panel.
- Notion's public Brand Guidelines page is a living page organized around logos, typography, color, illustration, photography, music, voice/tone, and key messaging. Its dynamic presentation means hidden spacing or palette details should not be inferred from search snippets; use the written trademark page and downloadable assets as the implementation authority.

Notion's written sources explicitly establish black/white treatment for the cube mark, but do **not** say that every partner icon should use a particular stroke width or line construction. CEDIA's line system must remain its own design decision.

## CEDIA design guardrails derived from the research

These are recommendations for the CEDIA mark, not claims that OpenAI or Notion require them.

1. Use a two-tone master: `#111` on light surfaces and `#fff` on dark surfaces. Keep the geometry identical between inversions; do not add gradients, shadows, textures, or provider colors to the mark.
2. Use one optical stroke family with round caps and joins. Make the negative space intentional and generous enough that the silhouette still reads at 16 px and 32 px. Check both light and dark backgrounds before choosing a direction.
3. Keep the mark semantically CEDIA-specific: open workspace channels, agent-to-agent handoff, concurrent flows, protocol gates, a folded route, or a routing compass. Do not use OpenAI's woven/knot-like Blossom construction, Notion's cube, or a generic six-loop interlock.
4. Keep the CEDIA mark primary in CEDIA product surfaces. If an integration is shown, use a supplied provider asset separately, preserve its exact form, add the required attribution when permission applies, and do not combine it into the CEDIA silhouette.
5. Test the final mark as a standalone app icon, a 16 px/32 px toolbar glyph, a light/dark SVG, and a mask. The final choice should be recognizable without color or a wordmark.

## Six original CEDIA directions

The names below describe the six candidate directions in the companion exploration set. They borrow the *principles* above (monochrome, geometric restraint, open space) while keeping different construction logic from both reference marks.

The historical open linework study referenced `output/brand-prototypes/cedia-open-linework-2026-09-28/README.md` for its editable SVGs, transparent PNG exports, and light/dark comparison. That generated output directory is absent from the current checkout. The visual assessments below are retained historical observations, not a fresh inspection of those missing artifacts.

| Direction | CEDIA meaning | Why it stays distinct | Main small-size check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **01 / Open channel** | Two opposing open arcs and staggered center channels: an accessible workspace and agent handoff. | The arcs are separate, with no closed woven knot or cube volume. Preserve both gaps. | The channels must remain distinct at 16 px. |
| **02 / Dual bridge** | Two side rails crossed by two links: human/agent collaboration and handoff. | Orthogonal rails and crossings create a bridge system rather than a radial or interlocking emblem. | Keep the two crossings legible after downsampling; avoid a solid rectangle. |
| **03 / Three currents** | Three parallel flowing paths: concurrent agents and work moving across surfaces. | Open horizontal currents have no enclosure, cube, or six-fold radial symmetry. | Maintain equal rhythm and separation; check that the waves do not alias. |
| **04 / Four gates** | Four open corner gates around a forward chevron: IDE boundaries, protocol admission, and send/route. | Detached corners and a directional chevron avoid Notion's closed cube and OpenAI's looped Blossom. | The chevron must remain visible beside the corner strokes at 16 px. |
| **05 / Folded route** | Nested angular paths fold toward a shared route: files, tools, and sessions crossing boundaries. | A path/routing construction with open terminals is materially different from a looped knot or cube. | Preserve the inner route and open ends; simplify if the nested paths fill in. |
| **06 / Split compass** | Two facing angular paths around a central diamond: routing, choice, and controlled direction. | The mark is bilateral and directional, not a woven interlock. Preserve the visible divide between its two paths. | The central diamond must remain a void/shape rather than a noisy dot. |

## Visual review of the six rendered candidates

This is a historical design assessment of the light/dark comparison board (`output/brand-prototypes/cedia-open-linework-2026-09-28/comparison.png`, absent from the current checkout), not a claim from the OpenAI guide or a trademark clearance. The board showed each mark at a large size and as 32 px and 16 px samples.

| Direction | Reference-language fit | Distinctive CEDIA cue | Small-size finding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 / Open Channel | Closest of the set to a calm, rounded, balanced app mark. | Separate side arcs and two horizontal channels; no continuous weave or six-fold loop. | The two channels and open ends remain readable at 16 px. |
| 02 / Dual Bridge | Bold, centered interconnection, but its two crossings bring it somewhat nearer the broad *idea* of interlocking. | Straight side rails give it a bridge silhouette and a human/agent reading. | The crossing becomes compact at 16 px but remains a distinct H-like sign. |
| 03 / Three Currents | Shares the mono stroke weight and rounded terminals; its horizontal rhythm differs strongly from the reference. | Parallel work or sessions. | Three lines remain separate, though the metaphor may be generic without a CEDIA wordmark. |
| 04 / Four Gates | Uses the same simple black/white confidence with a markedly different shape. | Workbench corners and a forward action form the strongest IDE-specific reading. | Corners and chevron stay distinguishable at 16 px. |
| 05 / Folded Route | Rounded technical path rather than a radial mark. | A route through bounded app surfaces. | The inner bend is crowded at 16 px; simplify before considering it for a toolbar. |
| 06 / Split Compass | Balanced and compact, with bilateral rather than radial construction. | Two choices around a central point. | The center diamond becomes a dot at 16 px; test a larger void or omit the diamond in a small-size variant. |

For an app-icon refinement, **01** best matches the requested monochrome, rounded visual feel while keeping a different construction; **04** is the clearest CEDIA-specific alternative. **02** is an option if the owner wants the human/agent handoff idea to dominate. These are recommendations from the rendered study, not a selected product mark.

### Second visual round after owner feedback

The owner asked whether the first six actually resembled ChatGPT's visual language. The honest assessment is **no**: they matched the color and stroke finish, but most read as familiar tool glyphs (waves, brackets, chevrons, and routes). The connected linework round revised all six with broad curved paths, overlap, and more compact centerward rhythm. Its historical files, `output/brand-prototypes/cedia-woven-monochrome-2026-09-28/README.md` and `output/brand-prototypes/cedia-woven-monochrome-2026-09-28/comparison.png`, are absent from the current checkout.

Visually, **02 Fourfold**, **04 Petal Gate**, and **05 Route Knot** are closest to the requested mood. Their four, four, and three path systems, square/petal/route centers, and open construction distinguish them from OpenAI's six-part Blossom. **01** reads as a bold circular emblem, **03** as a two-way exchange, and **06** as an asymmetric C. This is a visual distinction judgment, not evidence of exclusive originality or legal clearance. It should be checked again if one is selected for public use.

## Market similarity scan — 2026-09-28

At the time of this scan, I inspected the six SVGs and the rendered `output/brand-prototypes/cedia-woven-monochrome-2026-09-28/comparison.png` (now absent from the current checkout), then checked current first-party identity pages and a small image-search sample. “Similarity” below means visual family resemblance for design triage, not trademark clearance or a legal conclusion.

### First-party comparators

- [Windsurf brand guidance](https://windsurf.com/brand) publishes a standalone black/white symbol and asks users not to distort, outline, recolor, rotate, or add effects. The official [black symbol SVG](https://exafunction.github.io/public/brand/windsurf-black-symbol.svg) is a compact rounded, center-weighted mark; this makes the broad stroke and small central void in 01, 02, 04, and 05 a crowded visual territory.
- [Cursor's brand page](https://prod.cursor.com/cn/brand) presents a standalone cube and 2D/2.5D/3D app-icon system. The cube silhouette is unlike the CEDIA loops, but it shows how a developer tool uses a compact, high-contrast geometric mark at small sizes.
- [Tailwind's brand page](https://tailwindcss.com/brand) publishes the official mark and logotype. Its two opposing wave shapes are a useful first-party comparator for **03 Twin Currents**: the shared “paired flowing paths” idea is common even when the exact topology differs.
- [Woolmark's official history](https://www.woolmark.com/about/history/) identifies its interwoven emblem as the iconic Woolmark logo created in 1964. The observed three-loop/triskelion family makes **01 Trifold** especially vulnerable to generic interwoven-mark associations.

Two current app examples were also checked as market observations. The [AllChat site](https://askallchat.com/) serves a small [favicon](https://askallchat.com/favicon.png) that I observed as a green, four-ended branching glyph; it is not a looped knot. The [ChatNow App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ai-chat-assistant-chatnow/id1667518571) shows an icon I observed as four white rounded loops joined inside a black circle. These pages establish the products, while the shape descriptions are my visual observations from the rendered assets.

### Relative six-way ranking

| Rank for defensible distinctness | Candidate | Similarity pressure observed | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **06 Open Aperture** | Asymmetric nested C arcs and a directional notch avoid the radial knot family. Generic C, refresh, and arrow marks remain a broad category. | Best starting point; make the notch and arc spacing unmistakably CEDIA-specific. |
| 2 | **03 Twin Currents** | Two exchanged paths can read as wave, infinity, or chain; Tailwind is a direct paired-flow comparator. | Keep the unequal diagonal handoff and open terminals; avoid a symmetric infinity silhouette. |
| 3 | **02 Fourfold** | Four rounded bends around a center sit near generic knot, clover, and Celtic-loop marks. | Break perfect radial symmetry or enlarge the square void before public use. |
| 4 | **04 Petal Gate** | Four-petal/flower/clover marks are common; the observed ChatNow icon is a close current app example. | Change petal count, gate proportion, or directional behavior if selected. |
| 5 | **01 Trifold** | Three-loop/triskelion and interwoven-ring marks are saturated; Woolmark is a long-standing reference. | Needs a materially different opening or path rhythm to avoid generic triskelion reading. |
| 6 | **05 Route Knot** | Three-arm radial knot plus a central return cue is the closest of this set to familiar woven AI/assistant emblems. | Do not promote unchanged; alter topology and reduce the central knot before clearance. |

The image-search sample reinforced these pressure points: [three interlocking rings](https://www.vectorstock.com/royalty-free-vector/interconnected-circle-logo-concept-three-vector-40684900) are close to 01, and a [four-loop knot](https://www.jiffy.com/design/elegant-interlocking-knot-graphic-in-minimalist-black-and-white-16827812.html) is close to 02/04. A designer's [concentric C with a forward arrow](https://dribbble.com/shots/27267351-Letter-C-Arrow-Logo) shows that even 06's most distinctive idea is not wholly unused. These are discovery references only, not ownership or clearance evidence. Before release, run the selected mark through the official [Thailand DIP image search](https://tm-imagesearch.ipthailand.go.th/) and [trademark database](https://search-new.ipthailand.go.th/), plus counsel's broader jurisdictional search. This quick scan did not upload artwork, search every jurisdiction, or assess goods/services classes.

## Implementation and legal uncertainty

- The sources are live web pages and can change. Re-check the linked guides and current downloadable assets before release or external marketing.
- This research does not establish that CEDIA's six directions are legally clear. A designer or trademark counsel should perform a similarity search before publishing a selected mark, especially if a direction is later closed into a loop or combined with provider badges.
- The phrase “close to ChatGPT” should be interpreted as a request for monochrome discipline and simple geometric confidence, not permission to redraw the Blossom or use ChatGPT/OpenAI marks as CEDIA branding.
